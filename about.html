The site is held to the standards of an audit rather than those of a newsroom: every figure can be traced back to the reports it was calculated from, every method is published, and no news item or figure is edited by hand; notices from ATHENA News Room are marked as such and dated. What this is

ATHENA News Room is an automated news-intelligence service, not a newspaper: it publishes no reporting of its own. ATHENA INTELLIGENCE monitors the world’s English-language press around the clock — hundreds of verified newsrooms and official bodies from every region — and consolidates the reports of independent newsrooms into events. Each event is classified into one of ten intelligence domains and assessed on three published measures — importance, risk and confidence — with the evidence behind every number shown and every report linked to its source.

How to read the numbers

  • Importance (0–10): how much an event matters by its reach — how many countries and how many different outlets cover it, how fast it spreads, how new it is.
  • Risk (0–10): the potential harm the coverage describes — severity, urgency, knock-on effects. An analytical indicator, not a probability.
  • Confidence (%): how well the event is supported — corroboration by independent outlets, the reliability of the sources, stability.

Limits

It measures coverage, not truth: the English-language press only, and only the sources ATHENA reads. The methods are provisional and transparent.

How ATHENA publishes the news

ATHENA is built on one principle: show what independent newsrooms are reporting, and show the evidence behind every number. This section explains what we publish, how a story reaches the site, and why it works this way.

1. Collection

  • ATHENA reads 342 English-language news sources around the world, around the clock.
  • Every article is recorded with the country of the newsroom that published it, the countries it names, and its time.
  • Copies of the same agency report (Reuters, AP, AFP…) are counted once: ten papers running one wire story are one report, not ten.
  • Every new source is tested automatically before it is switched on; feeds that stop working are switched off.

2. Global news: verified events

  • Articles about the same development are grouped into one event, however many newsrooms report it.
  • Each event receives three scores, each with its reasons on the event page: importance (how widely and how fast it is covered), risk (how severe and urgent the harm described is) and confidence (how many independent, reliable sources stand behind it).
  • An event is published only when, within the last 48 hours, at least 2 independent outlets have reported it, its importance is at least 3, and it has a clear category.
  • The front page shows the 60 most important events; each event page stays online for 14 days so that links keep working.
  • The site is updated every 15 minutes; only the pages that changed are sent. The Telegram group receives breaking events with a risk of 6 or more, hourly round-ups and the daily briefing.

3. Local news: what the Local tab shows for each country

  • Its risk picture: the highest-risk verified event that is clearly about the country, how many are high-risk, and the status of its ports and straits.
  • Events about the country: the same verified events, filtered to those that are genuinely about that country (named in several of their articles), ranked by how much they concern it and by importance. This list follows the same rule of at least 2 independent outlets.
  • The country's own newsrooms: the latest headlines of the last 48 hours from outlets based in that country, linking to the original - in their own language, with a labelled machine translation into the page's language. This list is not cross-checked: it gives the local voice and speed, and a headline is marked when it is also a verified ATHENA event.
  • For about 10 of the most visited newsrooms of each covered country (EU, UK, USA, G20 to start), ATHENA's Local Desk reads the headlines in their own language and translates them offline into the site's languages; translations are labelled and the original is always shown. Today's brief and the main topics are drawn from these headlines; each newsroom is rated by kind and reach.

4. Why it works this way

  • Two independent outlets are the minimum standard of verification: a single report can be wrong, early or one-sided; independent confirmation is not.
  • ATHENA publishes an event only when at least 2 independent outlets report it. A country with 0–1 sources can never show its own local news, only international stories that mention it. This is why we keep adding tested newsrooms country by country: each one brings local stories closer to verified events.
  • Local headlines are shown separately, and labelled, so that readers get the immediacy of local reporting without confusing it with verified events.

5. Limits

  • Sources are English-language; countries with few English-language newsrooms are covered more thinly.
  • Places and topics are detected automatically and can occasionally be wrong.
  • Scores describe news coverage - not facts on the ground, forecasts or probabilities. Headlines and links belong to their publishers; ATHENA publishes no article text.
