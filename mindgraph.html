Mind Graph

Today's news as one network: every event on the front page, the countries it is about, the trackers that follow it and the actors events share. Ask the graph a question below it or click any node; the measures further down explain the picture.

Explore the network

Every dot is an event, country, section, tracker or shared actor. Lines show which belong together. Type a request under the graph and it shows you only that part of the network.

How to read this graph

Each symbol is one thing in today's news; a line means two things belong together - an event is about a country, sits in a section, is followed by a tracker or names an actor.

  • Event - a filled circle. Bigger = more important; colour = risk.
  • Country - a hollow ring. It grows with the number of events about it; the ring turns red when its events are high-risk.
  • Risk tracker - a thick gold ring for a long-running story (Hormuz, Red Sea, Taiwan …).
  • Section - a coloured square (conflict, economy, energy …). Events of that section hang around it.
  • Shared actor - a purple diamond: a person, organisation or place named in two or more events.

Risk colours: gold under 4 · amber 4–6 · orange 6–8 · red 8 and above (scale 0–10).

Three things to look for

  1. Tight clusters - the big stories of the day.
  2. A country ring with many lines - today's hub.
  3. An event sitting between two clusters - it links regions, often the one with the widest consequences.

How the Mind Graph is built

At every publish ATHENA takes the events of the front page and joins each one to the countries named in at least two of its articles (up to six), to its category, to the risk trackers that follow it, and to the actors it shares with other events. The measures are calculated from the same network. They describe news coverage, not facts on the ground, and they are not forecasts.

The Mind Graph

What you are looking at

  • Today's front-page events drawn as one network, joined to the countries, sections, risk trackers and actors they share.
  • Clusters are the big stories of the day; nodes that sit between clusters are the events that connect regions.

How to use it

  • Untick the Show buttons to hide a kind of node; set Minimum risk to keep only tense events.
  • Click a news dot to open the story (point at it first to preview its numbers); click a country, tracker or actor to list its events. Drag to move, scroll or pinch to zoom; Reset view brings everything back.
  • Type a question in the box under the graph, for example 'Iran and shipping', and only that part of the network stays lit.

Risk colours: gold under 4 · amber 4–6 · orange 6–8 · red 8 and above (scale 0–10).

ATHENA publishes an event only when at least two independent newsrooms report it.
